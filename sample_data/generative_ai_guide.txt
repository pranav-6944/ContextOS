# Generative AI & Vector Search Handbook

## Core Concepts of Neural Information Retrieval

### 1. Embeddings and Dense Representations
Unlike lexical keyword search (such as BM25), dense vector embeddings encode semantic relationships. Synonymous terms such as "cardiac infarction" and "heart attack" are located close to each other in 768-dimensional latent space, allowing natural language queries to retrieve relevant documents even without exact keyword matches.

### 2. Cosine Similarity & Distance Metrics
Cosine similarity measures the cosine of the angle between two multi-dimensional vectors. Given query vector Q and chunk vector C:
Similarity(Q, C) = (Q . C) / (||Q|| * ||C||)
For normalized vectors, this simplifies to the dot product. Scores range from -1.0 to +1.0, where 1.0 represents identical orientation in vector space. In ContextOS, chunks scoring above 0.40 are typically considered high-confidence matches.

### 3. Chunking Strategies and Trade-offs
- Small Chunks (100-300 characters): Higher precision during retrieval, but risk losing critical surrounding context.
- Large Chunks (1000-2000 characters): Retain full explanations, but dilute vector embeddings with unrelated sentences.
- Sweet Spot: 500-800 characters with 15-20% overlap provides an ideal balance for technical and academic texts.

### 4. Hallucination Mitigation
Retrieval-Augmented Generation substantially curtails LLM hallucination by forcing the model to answer strictly from the retrieved text. Citing explicit chunk references empowers human reviewers to verify every assertion against ground-truth source files.
